## Rolling Parts: Building the Car You Pictured

Parts aren't purchased — they're rolled at two separate machines using cash from completed runs. This is where having reference images of your target build pays off: you know exactly which engine, fuel tank, and weapons to prioritize before you start rolling.

Two principles from player experience shape smart rolling:

- **Treat Engines and Fuels as a pair.** A fast engine with an undersized tank ends your run just as fast as a slow engine. Fuel is the real early-game constraint, not top speed.
- **Watch for the "New distance record" banner.** It's the clearest signal your last build change worked. No banner? Revert and try a different combination.

## Skills: The Only Permanent Progress

Everything about your car resets between runs — except the Skills tree. According to community testing, here's the priority order:

| Priority | Skill | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | **Luck I** | Improves roll outcomes at both machines, multiplying the value of every cash you spend afterward |
| 2nd | **Luck II** | Stacks on Luck I — the roll improvement compounds |
| Later | **Gold Roll I** | Costs thousands of cash; a long-term goal, not an early buy |

Buy Luck I immediately after your first few runs. Because everything in the game flows through those roll machines, better luck improves every future build — including the parts you need to complete a blueprint.

## Timing Your Runs Around the Boss

Your HUD shows a "NEXT BOSS IN: XX:XX" timer on the right side from the moment you load in. Boss events are server-wide, so a poorly timed run means missing the fight entirely. Experienced players time their lobby phases around the clock: launch a run, return before the timer hits zero, and position yourself to respond to whatever the boss spawns. Stacking this habit with blueprint-based builds means more cash per session and faster progress toward better parts.
