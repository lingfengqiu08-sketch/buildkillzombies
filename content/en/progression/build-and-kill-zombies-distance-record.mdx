## Why Your Distance Record Matters More Than Anything Else

In Build and Kill Zombies, every run is judged by one number: how far you drove before running dry or getting torn apart. Your Build and Kill Zombies distance record is the clearest measure of whether your car actually works — beat it, and the game slaps a "New distance record" banner on your screen along with a fat payout of cash. Fail to beat it, and you've wasted a run that could have funded better parts.

The good news? You don't need hacker event items or Robux to set a serious record. Community-tested builds — including one documented run that reached 27,000 studs on a medium budget — prove that smart design beats expensive parts every time. This guide breaks down exactly how to build a distance machine that keeps rolling long after cheaper cars have become zombie chum.

## How the Run Loop Works (and What Ends a Run)

Every session follows the same tight loop: roll parts in the lobby, assemble your car on your plot, drive down the track into zombie waves, and earn cash based on distance covered. Then you spend that cash on better rolls and permanent skills, and go again.

Only two things end a run:

- **"Out of fuel"** — your tanks ran dry, full stop
- **"New distance record"** — you beat your previous best (this is the good ending)

Notice what's *not* on that list? Speed. A fast engine with an empty tank stops just as dead as a slow one. Fuel is the real constraint in this game, especially early on. If you internalize one idea from this guide, make it this: treat engines and fuel as a matched pair, not competing priorities.

## The 80/20 Rule: Fuel Beats Horsepower

The most reliable distance-record strategy circulating in the community is delightfully simple: **spam fuel**. Aim for roughly **80% of your build dedicated to fuel and 20% to engines** — and cap yourself at just two engines.

Here's why this counterintuitive setup works:

| Factor | All-Out Speed Build | 80/20 Fuel Build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fuel consumption | Extremely high | Very low |
| Speed stability | Erratic, engine overload | Balanced and steady |
| Distance potential | Dies early when dry | Runs for thousands of studs |
| Best engine placement | Anywhere | Front, to reduce weight |
| Budget required | High-tier parts | Medium budget works |

Two quality engines burn fuel efficiently and keep your speed balanced, while a mountain of fuel tanks keeps you driving. As player experience shows, when your rear tanks eventually get destroyed by zombies, your car gets *lighter* — which makes handling more precise and your effective speed even higher. Losing the back of your car isn't a death sentence; it's a second wind.

## The Carbon Fiber Distance Build: Part by Part

This community-sourced layout avoids hacker event items entirely and relies on carbon fiber parts for defense. Follow it roughly in this order:

| Step | Part | Placement | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Carbon fiber block | 8 blocks long (base frame) | Core chassis length |
| 2 | Tires | Along the frame | Movement |
| 3 | Carbon fiber log | Edges | Side defense against zombie attacks |
| 4 | Carbon cylinder | Back | Rear armor |
| 5 | Carbon fiber slab | Additional layering | Extra defense |
| 6 | Carbon fiber wedge | Top | Aerodynamics/protection on top |

Once the shell is done, the loadout matters even more:

- **Best fuel tank: front.** Zombies attack the rear first, and rear fuel barrels getting destroyed makes your remaining fuel drain faster. Keep your best fuel protected up front.
- **Best engine: front, and only two.** Reduces weight and keeps fuel consumption wasteful-free.
- **Driver seat: front.** If you sit in the back and the rear gets chewed up, you die before your parts do.
- **Guns: front and middle.** Mounting weapons underneath can cause the build to get stuck when you start driving, so keep them mid-mounted on the front section.

A player following this exact formula hit **27,000 studs on a medium budget** — no event exclusives required. The only shopping focus? Oil. Just keep buying fuel.

## What Happens Mid-Run (and Why Not to Panic)

The first few thousand studs look great, then zombies start chewing your rear. Here's the sequence, per community reports:

1. The back section takes damage first due to zombie attack delay.
2. Rear gas barrels break, temporarily increasing fuel drain.
3. As rear parts fall off, total weight drops.
4. The car becomes more precise and faster despite lower fuel.
5. The build stays balanced thanks to the game's core physics system.

The lesson: a destroyed rear isn't a failed run. Your front-loaded fuel and engine carry you through. Don't bail early — some of the biggest distance gains happen *after* the back half falls off.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good Build and Kill Zombies distance record for a medium budget?

Community reports put a well-built 80/20 fuel build at around **27,000 studs** using only medium-tier parts and no hacker event items. That's a strong benchmark — if you're below it, focus on adding more fuel and dropping to two engines.

### Why should I put fuel and the driver seat at the front of my car?

Zombies attack the rear first. If your driver seat is in the back, you die before your parts are destroyed. Similarly, rear fuel barrels break early and accelerate fuel drain — keeping your best tank up front protects your run's lifespan.

### How many engines should a distance record build use?

Two. More engines burn fuel faster without meaningfully extending distance. Two quality engines at the front keep speed balanced and fuel consumption low.

### Do I need Robux or event items to beat my distance record?

No. The carbon fiber build outlined above was documented reaching 27,000 studs without hacker event items. Free codes, the Luck skill tree, and smart fuel spam will carry you further than any cash pack.
